{{fig:crossing-closed}}

**Level crossings.** Drag a **Footpath** or **Road** across a straight rail line at a right angle (from the **Paths & Roads** drawer) and it becomes a level crossing: a footpath crossing costs $150 extra, a road crossing $400. A few seconds before a train arrives the barriers come down, red lamps blink and a quiet bell rings; guests and cars wait on the approach side and go on once the train has passed. Trains never stop for anyone, and nobody is ever run over. Crossings need a straight line, can't be next to another crossing (two tiles of room) or at a station, and a building can't stand on one. Bulldozing a crossing turns the tile back into plain rail.

{{fig:footbridge-rail}}

**Footbridge over the rail.** Switch on **Bridge over rail** in the Paths drawer and a footpath drawn across the line becomes a timber footbridge with stairs ($900) instead: guests climb over and never wait. Ranger Rudi suggests it when guests lose a lot of time at a crossing. (Roads always need a level crossing.)

{{fig:bridges-stream}}

**Bridges over water and gaps.** The **Bridge** tool in the same drawer builds from one bank across a stream, a pond or a ravine to the other bank: a **footbridge** for footpaths, a **wooden hiking bridge**, an **MTB bridge** and a one-lane **road bridge**. Spans are up to four tiles (road: three) in a straight line; the preview shows the span, deck height, supports and price, or the reason it is refused (for example *Bridges only cross water or a gap* or *Needs solid ground at both ends*). Bulldozing any tile of a bridge removes it and refunds half. Rail cannot cross water. On a touch screen the Bridge button works the same way, with large targets.
