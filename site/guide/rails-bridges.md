There is no bridge tool: bridges are part of drawing a path. Both the **Paths & Roads** and the **Pistes & Trails** drawers have a **Crossings** section that says what happens where: over rail you choose *Level crossing* (default) or *Bridge*; over water and gaps it is always a bridge, built automatically; where a path or road meets another one they simply merge.

{{fig:crossing-closed}}

**Level crossings.** Drag a **Footpath** or **Road** across a straight rail line at a right angle (from the **Paths & Roads** drawer) and it becomes a level crossing: a footpath crossing costs $150 extra, a road crossing $400. A few seconds before a train arrives the barriers come down, red lamps blink and a quiet bell rings; guests and cars wait on the approach side and go on once the train has passed. Trains never stop for anyone, and nobody is ever run over. Crossings need a straight line, can't be next to another crossing (two tiles of room) or at a station, and a building can't stand on one. Bulldozing a crossing turns the tile back into plain rail.

{{fig:footbridge-rail}}

**Footbridge over the rail.** Set **Crossings** to **Bridge** (in the Paths & Roads or the Pistes & Trails drawer) and a footpath or hiking trail drawn across the line becomes a timber footbridge with stairs ($900) instead: guests climb over and never wait. Ranger Rudi suggests it when guests lose a lot of time at a crossing. MTB trails get a longer ramp bridge ($1,100). (Roads always need a level crossing.)

{{fig:bridges-stream}}

**Bridges over water and gaps.** There is no bridge tool: draw as usual and drag across a stream, a pond, a lake or a ravine, and the stroke gets the matching bridge by itself: a **footbridge** for footpaths, a **wooden hiking bridge**, an **MTB bridge** and a one-lane **road bridge**. A stream counts like a pond: even a one-tile stream gets its bridge (the span-1 price of that type, shown in the preview as *over stream*); crossings from older saves become free bridges. Spans are up to four tiles (road: three) in a straight line (a trail that wobbles over the water is straightened for you, and a drag that stops in the water carries on to the far bank); it works the same in winter, when a frozen pond still needs its bridge. Ski pistes cannot cross water or rail. The preview shows the span, deck height, supports and price, or the reason it is refused (for example *Bridges only cross water or a gap* or *Needs solid ground at both ends*). Bulldozing any tile of a bridge removes it and refunds half. Rail cannot cross water. The **Crossings** section lists what happens where: over rail you choose, over water and gaps it is always a bridge, other paths and roads just merge.

**Bridges over rivers.** A river you dug (see [Building](page:building)) is real water like a stream: a footpath, hiking trail, MTB trail or road dragged across it gets the same bridge, at the same price, with *over stream* in the preview. A river cannot be filled in while a bridge crosses it (*A bridge crosses the river: remove the bridge first*); bulldoze the bridge first.

{{fig:bridge-suspension}}

**Suspension footbridge** (Career: resort level 3). When a footpath or hiking trail has to cross a river, a lake, a pond or a gorge that a regular bridge cannot span, the drag turns into a **suspension bridge** by itself, from 5 up to 12 tiles: towers on both banks, sagging cables (steel for footpaths, rope for hiking trails), a plank deck that dips a little in the middle, hand ropes and snow on the deck in winter. The preview names the span, the deck level and the price, **$400 a tile plus $800 for the anchors**, and there is no upkeep. If it cannot be built it says why (*Span too long: max 12 tiles*, *Too steep: the banks are too uneven for a suspension bridge*). Walkers cross it on the deck; **hikers on the trail version stop in the middle for the view**, so it counts as a viewpoint. Roads and MTB trails cannot use it.
