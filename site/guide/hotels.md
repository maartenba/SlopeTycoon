Two hotels sit in the **Lodging** tab of the Buildings drawer, next to the chalet and the apartment block, and a tiny **Coffee Stand** sits in **Services**. Hotels are bigger and dearer than a chalet; they only earn their keep once the resort has the guests to fill them.

## Small Hotel

{{fig:hotel-small}}

A mid-range house of **40 rooms** with a **lobby restaurant** (24 places, $12 a meal, open in the evening too): a bigger chalet with a restaurant under the same roof.

- **Cost**: $24,000, $55 a day, two staff. **Unlock**: resort level 3 (*Regional name*) in Career; Sandbox has it from the start.
- **Needs a road** next to it (no footpath alternative) and flat ground; auto-flatten does the rest.
- **Rate**: about $40 a night (the Pricing drawer has a slider for it like every lodging).
- Day guests dine in the lobby restaurant too, and guests look for it as an **evening venue**.

## Luxury Hotel

{{fig:hotel-luxury-night}}

**30 suites** at about **$100 a night** and a **heated outdoor pool** in front of the building. In cold air the pool **steams** (anything below 5 °C, thicker the colder it gets) and at night the windows and pool lights glow.

- **Cost**: $48,000, $90 a day, four staff. **Unlock**: resort level 4 (*Alpine destination*). **Needs a road**, flat ground.
- **Who sleeps here**: **VIPs fill the suites first**. Couples treat themselves too (about 4 in 10, a bit more in winter and at the weekend), and now and then a well-off solo guest or family does; they need enough in their wallet for four nights at the suite rate, **budget guests never take a suite**, and a VIP who cannot pay the rate goes elsewhere. A resort with plenty of couples fills roughly half its suites without any VIPs; the **VIP High-Rollers** campaign (see [Money](page:money)) or **Wellness & Views** (see [Seniors & school trips](page:seniors-schools)) fills the rest.
- **The pool**: 14 pool places on top of the usual rest places, overnight guests only. Every visit lifts the guest's mood a little more than an ordinary rest. The inspector shows *Rooms*, *Pool* use and the *Pool heating* cost.
- **Pool heating is energy**: about **$17 a day** at a winter noon (it never switches off entirely, even in summer: at least a quarter). It is booked on the **energy** line and counts in the sustainability facet; solar, wind, hydro and biomass offset it like the lifts' bill, at most 60 % of the total (see [Operations & safety](page:operations)).
- The **VIP campaign** now counts an open Luxury Hotel as its lodging requirement (an Altiport, or two chalets / apartments / small hotels, still count). Rudi suggests the hotel once the resort is busy and well rated.

> [!TIP] Put the hotel on a road near the lodge so guests walk to the slopes, and give it a toilet and a terrace nearby: seniors love a pool.

## Coffee Stand

{{fig:coffee-stand}}

A 1 x 1 kiosk for a quick cup and a bite: **$1,200**, **$4 a day**, one staff, four places, a $4 cup. It is **never gated**: available from the start. Place it **beside a piste, a footpath or a hiking trail** (not on it). A stand reached only by a trail or a piste edge is supplied on foot, which adds the usual 25 % to its upkeep.

- A cup takes the **edge off hunger** (about 0.3) and adds a little mood. It does **not** relieve the comfort urge: that stays the job of **Toilets**.
- **Skiers stop at the stand**: when they are deciding what to do next (lift bottom, end of a run, outside a building) a slightly hungry guest takes a coffee instead of walking to the lodge, and a skier who **skis past within two tiles** of an open stand, a little hungry or tired, will now and then pull over for a quick cup and then carry on. A really hungry guest still goes for a proper meal. Put stands where people pass or stand around: **beside a piste, at lift bottoms, the ends of pistes, beside terraces and shops**.
- **Hikers and bikers** treat a stand within reach as food, and buy a cup if it is the only food around.
- A thin wisp of steam rises from the stand's flue during the day, stronger in the cold.
- A stand is cheap, but it never replaces a lodge or a café: it only sells to people who are mildly hungry.
