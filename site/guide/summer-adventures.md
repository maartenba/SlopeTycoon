## Quad tours (level 2)

A **Quad Base** ($6,500) beside a road or footpath sends a convoy of four quads (8 riders, $40 each) out and back along it from 10:00 to 16:00 every two hours, with dust trailing behind and a very quiet engine drone. The route is found for you from the roads and footpaths that touch the base (never a trail or a piste). It closes in wind and snow and out of season. The engines scare wildlife and cost a little sustainability, never more than 3 points in total however many bases you have.

## Climbing crag (level 2)

{{fig:summer-crag}}

A **Climbing Crag** ($4,500) is a small hut at the foot of a **natural rock face**: the placement needs steep bare rock within three tiles and a footpath or hiking trail. Eight climbers at a time pay $30 a climb, helmeted and roped on the real rock. An **instructor** is optional: without one the crag stays open at half capacity and there are more twisted ankles, which the Ski Patrol answers (keep a patrol or first-aid hut in range). Nobody is ever badly hurt by a climb.

## Rafting and canyoning on a river (level 3)

{{fig:summer-rafting}}

Both bases follow a **waterway** downstream: a natural stream or a river you dug yourself (see [Rivers and springs](page:building)). The game finds the best stretch within three tiles of the base and the ghost shows it.

- **Rafting Base** ($5,500): two four-person rafts, $30 a ride, 10:00 to 16:00 every two hours. It needs a stretch of **brisk water or rapids** (the faster the water, the faster the raft; the ghost says *Needs a river or stream with brisk flow within 3 tiles* when there is none).
- **Canyoning Base** ($4,800): abseiling descents of a **steep, rocky stretch**, $35, at any flow. It needs a gorge (rock or steep banks along the stream).

Rapids thrill guests: they leave happier and find the price fairer. Both close in a **storm**, when the river **freezes** and in the **low water of late summer** (the inspector says which), and a rare twisted ankle brings out the patrol. After the ride a shuttle brings guests back. Neither costs sustainability. A river you dig works as well as a natural one: dig it from a spring on a slope so it runs brisk, and put the base beside it.

## Fishing

{{fig:summer-anglers}}

Put a **Tackle & Bait Hut** ($1,800, level 2) within three tiles of a lake, pond, stream or dug river. It sells day tickets ($12), rod hire and bait, and serves every water within 20 tiles. **Fishing Piers** ($400) along a lake or pond shore add four places each; shore tiles beside a footpath or trail work as bank spots. Every water has its own **fish stock**, which regrows by itself (faster in flowing water, never under ice). In the hut inspector choose:

- **Catch & release** (the default): the stock hardly shrinks.
- **Keep**: a little more income (the cafe buys the fish), but the stock drops about fifteen times faster.

When a water falls under 30 % the Advisor warns **Overfished** and anglers leave unhappy; switch to Catch & release or give the water a rest. Small ponds deplete faster than big lakes. A healthy stock adds a little to your sustainability score, an empty one takes a little away (at most two points).

In **winter** anglers **ice-fish** on frozen lakes and ponds: you see holes, stools and rods on the ice, no pier needed. Rivers and streams are never fished on the ice. New **Angler** guests come only when an open hut has fish in reach; they are quiet, spend little and keep coming. The *Angler's Paradise* campaign is on the [Money](page:money) page.
