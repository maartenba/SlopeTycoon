## Your guests

{{fig:slope-guests}}

Guests come as **solo** travellers, **couples**, **families**, **budget** skiers, **VIPs** and **adventurers**. Each type has its own taste in prices, its own patience and its own favourite things to do. In winter they ski or snowboard (roughly one in three arrives as a **Snowboarder**, see below), in summer they hike or ride mountain bikes, and some just relax at the lake or the rink.

Two **niche summer types** join them. **Mountain bikers** (lime shirt, helmet, about 15 % of the summer crowd) only come once the resort has a rideable MTB trail and an open lift that carries bikes; they ride the trails, rent bikes and count as adventurers for campaigns. **Anglers** (teal jacket and cap, quiet, low spend, steady) only come to a resort with an open Tackle & Bait Hut and fish in the water; they are the one type that also comes in winter, for ice fishing. More in [Summer](page:summer).

Two **calm types** fill the quiet times. **Seniors** (lilac cardigans, grey hair) come on weekdays and in the quiet seasons, tire quickly, need a toilet more often, never ride black runs and love a terrace, a café, a view or a hotel pool. **School groups** (one teacher and a class of 8 to 15 in orange bibs) arrive by coach on weekdays only, outside the holidays: in the first week of December, in January and on summer weekdays, and only to a resort with a ski school, kids ground or magic carpet (summer: a playground, petting zoo or campground). Both unlock at resort level 2: see [Seniors & school trips](page:seniors-schools). Overnight guests can now sleep in a **Small Hotel** or a **Luxury Hotel** (see [Hotels & the coffee stand](page:hotels)).

Two **winter types** round out the slope. **Snowboarders** (purple jackets, beanies, a board under the arm) are no longer just a look: about a third of the winter crowd (and a bit less in spring and autumn) are snowboarders, carved out of the solo, couple, budget and adventure arrivals, so the total number of guests stays the same. They love the **Stunt Park**, wide pistes and renting a board, and they dislike **long flat stretches and catwalks** where a board stops: a run full of flats loses them to a wide one. **Ski tourers** (red shells, a backpack, touring skis; from level 2) only come while the resort has an **open backcountry route below avalanche danger 4**; they skin up the route, spend little and love a mountain hut (see [Backcountry & avalanche danger](page:backcountry)).

{{fig:guests-boarders-tourers}}

Guests have needs: they queue for lifts, get hungry, tire, get cold and sometimes get hurt, and sooner or later they need a toilet. When they leave they judge the resort, and the average becomes your **star rating**.

### Toilets and the comfort need

{{fig:toilets-hut}}

Every guest has an **urge** that grows while they are out and about (a bit faster when walking than when standing still). Above about half, the guest looks for the nearest open **Toilets** (Buildings drawer, $600, $3 a day, no staff, free to use) and walks there; eating at a café, hut or restaurant also eases it. A guest who cannot find one never leaves or gets hurt, but their mood sags a little and a review may say so ("Not a toilet in sight"). Put toilets where people are: beside the village footpath, near the base lodge and next to busy pistes (a toilet may stand at a **piste edge** or on a hiking trail as well as on a footpath). One block serves about 70 guests a day; past that it is *overrun*, stays unpleasant and counts for nothing. A career game starts with one free toilet near the village, and Ranger Rudi nags when there are none or when too many guests are desperate. How well you do is the **Facilities** bar of your rating (below, and in [Events & the region](page:events)).

### Reviews

The **News** panel shows guest reviews in their own words: quiet slopes, long gondola queues, great hot chocolate. Treat them as free consulting.

### Click a guest

Click any guest to open the **guest inspector**: type, mood, what they are doing and a short bio. From there you can **Ride along** (see [Ride, Walk & Photo](page:modes)). A few guests carry a name and a wish; see [Events, stories & the region](page:events).

## The year

A game year has **180 days** in 12 months of 15 days, and the months are the **real ones** (northern hemisphere). A new game starts on **1 December**. The date is always visible in the top bar as a **date chip** such as *Sat 14 Feb · Winter · Year 1* (hover it for the day of the year and the time of day). The same real dates appear in the speed panel, the news, the month cards, the year report and the save list.

### Calendar & seasons

| Season | Months | Length | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winter | December to March | 60 days | Peak ski season: queues, grooming nights, holidays. Coldest in mid-January. |
| Spring | April and May | 30 days | The snowline climbs. Late March and April are spring skiing: slush in the afternoons, low pistes melt out early in April, high snow lasts into June. |
| Summer | June to September | 60 days | Hiking, biking, lakes and farms. Lifts that carry bikes shine. Warmest in mid-July. |
| Autumn | October and November | 30 days | A breathing space: maintenance, bears get hungry, the first snow on the peaks in October and in the valley in late November. |

Daylight follows the sun too: the **longest day is 21 June** and the **shortest 21 December**, so December days are short (lifts open about 08:30) and summer days long. Weekdays are real as well: Saturday and Sunday are the peak days (see [Money](page:money)), and quiet weekdays are when **Seniors** and **school classes** visit: the first week of December and January are school-class weeks in winter (see [Seniors & school trips](page:seniors-schools)).

Festivals sit in their real months: Christmas Market in December, Winter Carnival in February, World Cup in January and February, Music Festival from June to August, the Almabtrieb in early October and New Year on 31 December. The **Events strip** in the Marketing drawer shows all twelve months at a glance (see [Events, stories & the region](page:events)).

### The real snowline

Snow depends on **altitude and temperature**. Low pistes melt out first, high ones last, which is why **spring skiing** works only up high while the valley already hikes. Buildings show a *closed: too warm at 1,240 m* message when the air is wrong for them. **Snowmaking** buys you time on a thin base (see [Lifts & pistes](page:lifts-pistes)); you can switch the cannons off yourself or let them pause when it is too warm.

### Summer is not a holiday

Winter buildings are mothballed in summer and cost a fraction of their upkeep, but you still pay wages, loans and the rest. Build hiking and biking trails, an Adventure Park, the beach or boat rental, a campground, a petting zoo, a viewpoint, and run a summer campaign. The [Summer](page:summer) page is a plan: campground, petting zoo, viewpoint, treehouse, quads, a climbing crag, rafting and canyoning, and fishing. Rudi warns you in late spring to save a buffer.

{{fig:season-summer}}

On a lift with **mid-stations** (see [Lifts & pistes](page:lifts-pistes)), hikers and bikers who ride up choose the station **nearest their goal** and walk the trails from there, so a mid-station works as a trailhead half way up the mountain. A **suspension footbridge** on a hiking trail is a viewpoint: hikers stop in the middle for the view, and so is a **Viewpoint** platform or a **Treehouse**: build them on high ground or in a forest beside a trail and hikers walk up for the view, stop longer and leave happier. **Summer arrivals** come in with the warm weather: lifts that carry bikes, trails, lodging that opens in summer (the campground) and attractions bring more guests, and a resort with none of them still gets a thin stream of hikers.

Hikers get hungry too. Before a trip they eat if they can, about four in ten bring a free packed lunch, and a hut, café, terrace or the farm shop within about eight tiles of the route counts as a **food stop** on the way. If a trail is long and has no food anywhere near it, guests shorten or skip the trip, and Ranger Rudi suggests *Hikers are going hungry*: add a hut or café near the trails.

## Day and night

Guests ski between morning and the late afternoon. At night windows glow, lamp posts light and **groomers** go to work. The moon button on the speed controls makes nights pass about three times faster; while it is on, a small badge sits on the moon button.

## Time controls

One in-game day lasts **150 seconds** at normal speed (1×). The speed buttons:

| Button | Day lasts about |
|---|---|
| Pause (<kbd>Space</kbd>) | stopped (you can still build) |
| 1× | 150 s |
| 3× | 50 s |
| 10× | 15 s |

The speed panel shows today's real date under the buttons (hover for the full date and day of the year). Use <kbd>+</kbd> / <kbd>.</kbd> for faster and <kbd>-</kbd> / <kbd>,</kbd> for slower.

### Skip to…

The skip button (next to the speed controls), or <kbd>K</kbd>, fast-forwards: **Next month**, **Next season** or **Next event**. It stops by itself when something important happens, and any click, <kbd>Esc</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> cancels it. It is not available in ride-along, walk or photo mode, or in the tutorial.

> [!TIP] Waiting for money to build the next gondola? Skip a month, then read the monthly summary.
