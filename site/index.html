What is it?

A ski-resort tycoon with a real mountain under it

You start with a valley, a lodge and a little money. Raise and flatten terrain, dig ponds and plant gardens, paint pistes, build eleven kinds of lifts, add restaurants, ski schools and ranger stations, and watch tiny guests actually ski your slopes. The snowline moves with the seasons, so the mountain you build for winter has to earn its keep in summer too.

How it plays

Three steps, then a lot of “one more season”

Build

Lay a road, add a lift and paint a piste down the hill. Ranger Rudi and the goals card point at what to do next. Getting started guide.

Open & watch

Guests arrive, queue, ski, eat and write reviews. Zoom in, ride along on a lift or walk through the village yourself.

Grow

Price your passes, run campaigns, take a loan, level up and unlock gondolas, funiculars and helipads. Then do it all again in summer. How money works.

Only here

Things you won’t find in other resort games

Everything is procedural

Every tree, building, texture, sound effect and piece of music is generated by code at runtime. The only files are two fonts.

Walk your own resort

Press V and drop into first person. Stroll the village, board a lift, take photos from eye level.

A real snowline, a real calendar

Snow depends on altitude and temperature, and the months are the real ones: winter December to March, longest day on 21 June. Spring skiing happens high up while the valley hikes. Plan for it or go broke in April.

Pistes are runs

Every stretch of piste between two junctions is its own run, with a name, difficulty, traffic and an open / close switch. Skiers choose between them.

Green energy, with a catch

Solar, wind, hydro and biomass take a capped share off your lift energy bill and raise your sustainability score. Energy is never free, so you do the maths. Late game, the Eco Centre adds a funded Eco programme with a carbon-neutral certificate.

Plays offline

Install it like an app. After the first visit the whole game and your saves work without a connection.

No ads, no accounts, private saves

Saves live on your device (export them as .slope files) and are never uploaded. The only thing that can be sent is anonymous usage statistics, and only if you allow it. Privacy & analytics.

Seasons & snowline

Four seasons. One valley. Very different jobs.

A year lasts 180 days in twelve real months (a new game starts on 1 December) with real weather, snowfall and a snowline that creeps up the mountain in spring. Winter, December to March, is for skiing, spring for high-altitude “spring skiing”, summer for hiking and mountain biking, autumn for getting ready. A date chip in the top bar always tells you where you are.

  • Snowmaking cannons and a reservoir keep the base open in thin years, and you can switch cannons off or let them wait for the cold.
  • Day and night: groomers work the slopes after dark, windows glow.
  • Time your way: 1×, 3× and 10× speeds, plus “Skip to…” the next month, season or event.

Lifts & pistes

Eleven kinds of lifts, towers and all

From a humble T-bar to a ten-person gondola, a cable car and a funicular. Lifts get towers that follow the terrain and real clearance rules: cables must clear trees, roofs and other lifts, so you decide where to cut a forest lane.

  • Pistes by difficulty colour, split into runs you can rename, close and groom one by one.
  • Grooming and snowmaking keep them in shape.
  • Planned maintenance: every lift needs its annual service, or it becomes overdue and breaks down more.

Living guests

Every guest is an actual person on an actual slope

Families, couples, budget skiers, VIPs and adventurers. Skiers, snowboarders, hikers and bikers, each with their own gear, build and jacket. They queue, eat, get tired, get hurt and then tell you about it in their reviews.

  • Eight skin tones, nine head styles, sixteen jacket colours: a crowd that actually looks like a crowd.
  • Reviews on the news feed tell you what to fix.

Mountain life

Run a resort, not just a slope

Ski patrol sleds, trained crews, first-aid huts and a rescue helicopter keep guests safe. Rare avalanches and storms close slopes. Bears wake in autumn, wolves roam the remote forest, and rangers keep everybody apart. Dramatic, never gory: nobody dies in this game.

  • Patrol, first aid, helipad coverage on a map overlay.
  • Wildlife you can manage with a ranger station and bear-proof bins.
  • Energy & sustainability: solar arrays, wind turbines, small hydro, biomass, a tree nursery and a recycling centre.

Festivals, rivals & people

A valley that has something going on

Book a Christmas Market, a Winter Carnival, a Summer Music Festival or a World Cup race and watch the village dress up: bunting, banners, market stalls, processions and a grandstand. The Almabtrieb brings the decorated cows down in early October, announced days ahead. Neighbouring resorts compete for the same guests, and some guests arrive with a name and a wish of their own.

  • Statistics (I): charts of guests, cash, rating and revenue over years, and every mountain has a named region and peaks.
  • Six staff courses and four crews, all optional and automatic by default.
  • Sustainability counts in your rating, and a Green Resort award waits in the year report.
  • Undo and redo for everything you build, a slope profile while you draw and a Suggest a run helper.

Career, scenarios & trophies

Goals to chase, stories to finish

In Career mode your resort levels up from “Open for business” to a world-class destination, unlocking bigger lifts along the way. Prefer a challenge? Six handcrafted scenarios, from a gentle first valley to the Alpine Giant. Every year closes with a Resort of the Year report, and {{achievements}} trophies unlock cosmetic rewards like new building palettes and photo frames.

Economy

Prices, passes, loans and the occasional marketing blitz

Set day-pass and season-pass prices, vary them by weekday and season, and see at once whether a price is cheap or expensive. Run campaigns for families, budget skiers, VIPs, summer adventurers, snowboarders, hikers and bikers, or tempt people onto the train with Come by Train, once you have what they need. Build toilets, bridges and level crossings so the valley works. Take a loan, mind the debt, and don’t forget summer still has to pay for itself.

About

Why I built this

I am Maarten. I like skiing, and every time I came back home from a family ski vacation I wanted the week in the mountains and on the slopes to last longer. Around the year 2000 I started playing Ski Resort Tycoon after the vacation to do just that.

And then summer vacations in the mountains, same thing… There was no summer ski town game, I believe, but it’s something I would have liked. Back then, I also swore that “one day, I’ll build a game like this”. Thanks to AI assistance, here we are!

FAQ

Questions

Is it free? Do I need an account?

Yes it is free, and no, there are no accounts and no ads. Saves stay on your device.

Is anything tracked?

Only if you allow it: anonymous usage statistics (Google Analytics) are off until you click “Allow”, and never include names, saves or text. See Privacy & analytics.

Does it work on my phone or tablet?

Yes, with touch support: one-finger pan, pinch to zoom, two-finger twist to rotate, a pan / paint toggle and a two-step placement so nothing is built by accident. Tablets are comfortable and phones are playable, though the game is still best on a desktop with a mouse. See Touch & tablets.

Can I play offline?

Yes. Open the game once while online and it caches itself. Install it as an app for a home-screen icon.

Where are my saves?

In your browser’s storage on this device. Use Save / Load → Export to keep a .slope file as a backup or to move to another computer.

Does the game use real months?

Yes. A year is 180 days in twelve real months, northern hemisphere: winter December to March, spring April and May, summer June to September, autumn October and November. Festivals, daylight and the snowline follow that calendar.

Is it finished?

It is a pre-release (v{{version}}). Expect rough edges, and please check the changelog for what changes.
