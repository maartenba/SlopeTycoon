## Why I wanted a summer game

When I came home from family holidays in the mountains, I wanted the week to last. That was true after winter trips, and it was just as true after summer ones. I could never find a game about the summer half of a mountain town: the hiking, the campsites, the lakes, the small events in the village. So Slope Tycoon has one.

The snow line moves with the real months. By April the high slopes still ski while the valley hikes. If you only plan for winter, the lifts stand idle and the loan keeps costing money.

## What you can build

- **Trails.** Hiking and mountain bike trails that can be painted all year. Bridges are built for you when a trail crosses water.
- **A campground** with a campfire that glows at night.
- **A petting zoo**, where kids buy a scoop of feed at the gate and the animals walk to the fence.
- **A viewpoint and a treehouse**, which hikers stop at for the view.
- **Quad tours** that drive convoys on your roads.
- **A climbing crag** on a real rock face.
- **Rafting and canyoning** on a river you dug from a spring.
- **Fishing.** A tackle and bait hut, piers, a fish stock that regrows, and catch and release or keep. In winter you can ice fish on a frozen lake.

{{fig:summer-campground-night}}

## Guests and campaigns

Mountain bikers use your trails and bike-carrying lifts. Anglers come to the water and stay calm. Two summer campaigns bring them in: Family Summer Camp and Angler's Paradise. Hikers and bikers get hungry, so you need food near the trails, and toilets help everyone.

## Events

A Summer Music Festival fills the village, and with a gondola or cable car you can book a Mountain Concert in early September: a stage with lights that sway over the crowd at night. The Almabtrieb, the cows coming down in early October, marks the end of the season.

{{fig:concert-summer-night}}

## A plan for your first summer

1. Check which of your lifts carry bikes. Chairs and gondolas do, T-bars do not.
2. Paint one hiking trail and one bike trail from the top station.
3. Put food and a toilet where the trails meet.
4. Add a campground or a viewpoint, then see how the guests react.
5. Dig a river if you want rafting. Place the spring first.
