## The basic idea

A haul rope runs in a loop between two stations. At one end, usually the top for a chairlift, a big wheel called the bull wheel is turned by a motor and pulls the rope. At the other end a second wheel keeps the rope tight. Along the way the rope sits on rollers on steel towers, which hold it up above the ground. Whatever carries you is attached to the rope: a chair, a cabin or a bar. When the rope moves, you go uphill.

That is all there is to it. The interesting part is the station, because getting on and off a moving rope safely is what shapes everything else.

## Fixed-grip chairlifts

On an older chairlift each chair is clamped to the rope permanently. The rope never slows down, so the speed has to be low enough to sit on a passing chair safely, around two metres a second or a little more. That limits capacity. Two- and four-seat fixed chairs are cheap to build and fine for a beginner slope or a short line.

In Slope Tycoon: the 2-seat and 4-seat fixed chairlifts. They are the cheap chairs, and they cannot have mid-stations.

## Detachable chairlifts

A detachable chair has a grip that opens in the station. The chair comes off the rope, rolls slowly along a rail while you sit down, and then the grip clamps onto the rope again and the chair speeds up to line speed. Because the rope itself can run at five or six metres a second while the chairs crawl through the station, these lifts are quicker and carry more riders.

Some are fitted with a bubble, a clear cover you pull down over your head, which makes a cold day much nicer. A six-seat bubble chair is a common sight in big resorts.

In Slope Tycoon: the 4-seat detachable, 6-seat detachable, 6-seat high-speed, 6-seat bubble and 8-seat bubble chairlifts. They load faster than the fixed ones, and they can be given mid-stations.

## Gondolas

A gondola is a detachable lift with closed cabins. The cabins also let go of the rope in the station, slow down, and let you step in. It is warm, you can take your skis in with you or put them in a rack on the outside, and bikes in summer. Gondolas are used for long, steep lines and for bringing people up to the mountain in summer.

In Slope Tycoon: the 6-person and 10-person gondolas. They reach further than a chair, and they can have mid-stations.

## T-bars and other surface lifts

A T-bar is a surface lift: you stay on the snow and the lift pulls you up the slope. Two people share a T-shaped bar, which you place behind your thighs. You do not sit on it. Rope tows and button lifts work the same way. They are cheap, run on short slopes and are common on beginner areas.

In Slope Tycoon: the T-bar. It is cheap, reaches only a short distance and cannot be given mid-stations.

## Cable cars

A cable car, or aerial tramway, is different. Two big cabins hang from fixed track ropes and are pulled by a separate haul rope. When one goes up, the other comes down, and they pass each other half way. A cabin can carry from a few dozen up to over a hundred people. Cable cars can cross huge gaps and reach very high stations, but each cabin only leaves every few minutes.

In Slope Tycoon: the Cable Car, with a cabin of 40 people, a very long reach and a big price. It stops at every station, so a mid-station lowers its capacity.

## Funiculars

A funicular runs on rails up a slope, with two cars connected by a cable so that the weight of the one going down helps pull up the other. It works in all weather and can run through tunnels, which is why you often find them in towns and under steep terrain.

In Slope Tycoon: the Funicular, which carries 60 people per car and is the one lift that wind never closes. It cannot have mid-stations and does not carry bikes.

## Riding a lift for the first time

- **Chairlift:** hold your poles in one hand, look over your shoulder at the chair, sit when it arrives. Lower the safety bar. At the top, lift the bar before the ramp, stand up and ski away straight.
- **Snowboard:** unclip your back foot, and keep it on the stomp pad or the board. Slide out of the chair like you would on skis.
- **Gondola:** take your skis off, carry or rack them, and step in. Step out the same way.
- **T-bar:** place the bar behind your thighs, keep your skis in the track and let it pull you. Do not sit down. At the top, step out of the track and let go.
